Arkansas campaigns cannot organize effectively with incomplete or outdated voter information.

Campaigns and county organizations are expected to identify supporters, contact voters, recruit volunteers, raise money, and turn people out to vote.

But too often, they are trying to do that work with information that is incomplete, outdated, inconsistent, or difficult to use.

Many organizations rely heavily on VAN. VAN remains an important organizing tool, but the information available inside any voter-contact system is only as useful as the data feeding it.

Common problems include:

  • Missing or outdated phone numbers
  • Missing mobile numbers for texting
  • Incomplete email information
  • Old or incorrect mailing addresses
  • Duplicate or disconnected voter records
  • Valuable campaign contacts stored in spreadsheets, phones, notebooks, or separate systems
  • Local knowledge that never becomes part of a usable campaign database

Platform subscribers already work from important Arkansas voter information—current voter-registration records from the Secretary of State—plus contacts and notes the campaign provides.

That gives every campaign a usable foundation without joining the Data Cooperative.

For verified emails, mobiles, and enhanced addresses at scale, the cooperative file is still the upgrade.

A stronger statewide data foundation

We are working to acquire a professionally enhanced Arkansas voter and consumer data file containing approximately 1.6 million records.

The enhanced file includes, where available:

  • Verified email addresses
  • Verified mobile and text-capable numbers
  • Verified telephone numbers
  • Current mailing addresses
  • Voter-registration information
  • Geographic and district information
  • Additional contact information that can help campaigns reach the people they are trying to serve

This data can help campaigns and county organizations improve the information they already have, update their internal systems, and strengthen the records they use in VAN or other organizing platforms.

The goal is not to replace every system currently being used.

The goal is to make those systems better.

Keep using VAN—just make the information stronger

Organizations that rely on VAN can use the enhanced information to identify missing contact fields, correct outdated records, and improve their ability to communicate with voters.

Depending on the technical and licensing requirements of the systems involved, the data can be prepared for matching, updating, appending, or importing into the organization’s existing workflow.

That means campaigns do not have to abandon the tools they already know.

They can strengthen them.

Your campaign contacts remain your campaign contacts

Every campaign builds information that belongs to that campaign.

That may include:

  • Supporter identifications
  • Volunteer relationships
  • Donor prospects
  • Yard-sign requests
  • Event attendees
  • Personal voter conversations
  • Local leaders
  • Community relationships
  • Notes from doors, calls, texts, and meetings

Participating campaigns can add their own contacts and voter information to the platform while keeping those records separated from other campaigns and organizations.

A campaign’s private voter contacts are not automatically placed into a shared statewide pool.

They can be firewalled and visible only to the campaign or organization that created them.

Optional: Cooperative data pricing

The statewide enhanced data file costs approximately $24,000. This is separate from the $149 monthly Electd platform subscription.

Without cooperative participation, platform users work with Electd/SOS registration data plus records the campaign provides. Rather than requiring one organization to absorb the full enhanced-file cost, we are building a cooperative purchasing model that spreads acquisition across participating campaigns and county organizations.

Local races

$200 one-time data contribution

Justice of the Peace and other races smaller than a county. Receive enhanced information for the relevant local geography.

Plus $149 per month for campaigns using the Electd communications platform.

County organizations

$300 one-time data contribution

Counties can receive the enhanced information for their county and use it to improve their own records and existing organizing systems.

The $149 monthly Electd platform subscription is optional for counties that only want the data.

State House and State Senate

$500 one-time data contribution

Plus $149 per month for campaigns using the Electd communications platform.

Statewide campaigns

$700 one-time data contribution

Plus $149 per month for campaigns using the Electd communications platform.

How the cooperative model works

If 50 counties participate:

50 counties × $300 = $15,000

That covers more than 60% of the statewide data acquisition.

If three statewide campaigns and ten local races participate:

3 statewide × $700 = $2,100

10 local races × $200 = $2,000

That brings the cooperative total to:

$19,100

Only ten State House or State Senate campaigns would then be needed to complete the purchase:

10 legislative campaigns × $500 = $5,000

Total: $24,100

This model allows campaigns and county organizations to receive significantly better information at a fraction of what it would cost any one participant to purchase the statewide file independently.

What participants receive

Participants receive access to the portion of the enhanced data appropriate for their campaign, district, county, local geography, or statewide jurisdiction, subject to the final vendor licensing agreement.

They can use the information to:

  • Improve voter-contact records
  • Update internal campaign databases
  • Strengthen VAN records and matching
  • Build more accurate call, text, email, and mailing lists
  • Reduce wasted communication costs
  • Reach voters who are missing from current outreach lists
  • Preserve campaign-specific relationships
  • Improve organizing before Election Day
  • Leave the campaign with a stronger, more useful contact system

The opportunity

Campaigns should not have to spend thousands of dollars building separate systems, buying separate data, and paying for multiple disconnected communication tools.

This cooperative model gives campaigns access to:

  • Better statewide data

Founding participation is conditional

The reduced cooperative pricing is available only if enough campaigns and organizations participate to fund the statewide data acquisition.

Commitments will be collected until the cooperative reaches the required funding threshold.

The statewide file will not be purchased—and the discounted pricing will not be finalized—unless the participating organizations collectively cover the acquisition cost.
